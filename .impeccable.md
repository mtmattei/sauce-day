# Design Context — Sauce Day

## Target audience
Five men in Montreal who have made tomato sauce together every August since 2020.
Matt (host, owns the gear), David (drinks, hardware runs), Nate (market, bakery),
Chris (breakfast run, grocery), Mike (focaccia). Non-technical. They read this on
phones, outdoors, with tomato on their hands. Matt also reads it at a desk in
February when he is planning.

## Use cases
- Planning (Feb–Aug): price the ledger, decide bushels, decide what to buy.
- The week before: shopping list by store, by buyer.
- The day itself (Aug 29): run sheet, menu, tick things off.
- 22:30 that night: settle up, log the grappa, close the year.
- Forever: the historical record. 2020 onward, one row per year.

## Brand personality / tone
Naoto Fukasawa × Josh Puckett. Fukasawa gives the object-ness: one surface, one
gesture, nothing decorative, no branding, "without thought." Puckett gives the
data: dense real numbers, editorial typography, tabular figures, calm at high
information density.

Not cute. Not rustic-Italian. Not a recipe blog. It is an instrument for a
recurring annual operation that happens to be about tomatoes.

## Palette
Monochrome (warm-tinted neutrals, never pure black or white) with a single
tomato accent. Tomato is scarce and always means something specific: money out,
still to buy, or the record to beat. If it appears without meaning, remove it.

## Tagline
"cut the bad" — literally what you do to a tomato before it goes in the pot, and
the design rule for the whole interface. Subtraction is the method.

## Constraints
Static, no build step, no framework. Works on a phone held sideways in a
backyard. Real data only — the numbers come from the 2020–2025 sheets.
